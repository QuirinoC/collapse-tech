# Procedure Cost Comparison — Mexico vs. United States

> **Internal reference document.** All figures are **illustrative estimates compiled from publicly reported rates** (hospital price lists, medical tourism industry reports, and US healthcare cost transparency data). These are **not quotes** and must not be presented to customers as quotes. Actual prices vary by provider, city, case complexity, and year.

## Assumptions used for trip costs

| Component | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Round-trip flight | $300–$700 per person from a major US hub to Tijuana, Monterrey, Guadalajara, or Cancún |
| Accommodation | $70–$140/night, mid-range hotel or recovery-friendly Airbnb near the clinic |
| Ground transport | $50–$150 total (airport transfers + clinic visits; Uber is cheap in these cities) |
| Meals & incidentals | $30–$60/day |
| Companion | Optional; adds roughly flight + half of accommodation/meals. Shown separately where relevant |
| Stay length | Dental/cosmetic/hair: 4–7 nights. Bariatric: 5–7 nights. Orthopedic: 7–10 nights (inpatient 2–3) |

**US cost scenarios explained:**

- **Cash / self-pay:** The "surgical package" rate hospitals quote uninsured patients. This is what you pay with no insurance at all.
- **In-network PPO (good scenario):** Deductible met + coinsurance (typically 20% after deductible), capped by out-of-pocket maximum (~$9,450 individual in 2025 for ACA plans). Assumes the surgeon, anesthesiologist, AND hospital are all in-network — often not the case.
- **High-deductible plan (common scenario):** HDHP with $6k–$8k deductible means the patient pays nearly the full negotiated rate before insurance kicks in.
- **Out-of-network / surprise billing (bad scenario):** Any provider in the chain that's out-of-network (frequently the anesthesiologist or assistant surgeon) bills balance charges on top. Pre-2022 this was catastrophic; the No Surprises Act caps emergency and some incidental billing, but elective surgery at an out-of-network facility remains fully exposed.

## Detailed trip-cost breakdowns

### 1. Gastric sleeve (sleeve gastrectomy) — Tijuana / Monterrey

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Surgeon + hospital fee (often packaged) | $4,500–$7,000 |
| Pre-op labs & endoscopy (if not bundled) | $150–$400 |
| Flights (RT, patient) | $300–$500 |
| Hotel (5–7 nights × $90) | $450–$650 |
| Ground transport + meals | $250–$400 |
| **Total** | **≈ $5,650–$9,350** |

Most Mexican bariatric providers sell an all-inclusive package that already bundles hospital, surgeon, anesthesia, 1–2 hospital nights, hotel, and ground transfers — which is why bariatric is the #1 medical tourism category.

### 2. Dental implants & full-arch restoration — Los Algodones ("Molar City") / Tijuana / Cancún

| Item | Single implant | All-on-4 (per arch) |
|---|---|---|
| Implant + abutment + crown | $1,300–$2,100 | $8,000–$12,000 |
| Flights RT | $300–$600 | $300–$600 |
| Hotel (4–7 nights × $80–$120)* | $320–$840 | $560–$840 |
| Transport + meals | $150–$300 | $200–$350 |
| **Total** | **≈ $2,100–$3,800** | **≈ $9,000–$13,800** |

\* Implants require 2 trips ~3–6 months apart (placement then restoration); many border-city clinics do single-visit immediate-load implants to avoid the second trip. Budget accordingly.

### 5. IVF — Guadalajara, Monterrey, Cancún, Mexico City

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| IVF cycle (egg retrieval + fresh transfer) | $4,000–$6,500 |
| Medications (stimulation protocol) | $1,500–$3,000 |
| ICSI / PGT-A genetic testing (optional add-ons) | $1,000–$2,500 |

US comparison: $12k–$25k per cycle before meds; meds add $3k–$7k; most patients need 2–3 cycles. Cash-pay IVF in the US commonly totals $40k–$80k to a live birth.

## Why the US numbers vary so much (the four scenarios)

1. **Uninsured/cash-pay** pays inflated chargemaster-derived "package" rates, though cash discounts of 30–50% exist if negotiated. This is the baseline most medical tourists compare against.
2. **Insured in-network** looks cheapest on paper ($3k–$9k patient share after deductible/coinsurance/OOP-max) **but requires**: prior authorization, in-network surgeons AND facilities AND anesthesia, medical necessity documentation. Denials or one OON link in the chain push patients toward scenario 3 or 4.
3. **High-deductible plans** (now >30% of employer coverage): the patient eats the entire negotiated rate until hitting a $6k–$8k deductible — often close to cash price anyway.
4. **Out-of-network/surprise-billing**: worst case. Elective surgery at any OON facility has no federal protection; balance billing can double or triple the bill. Even in-network hospitals routinely produce surprise OON anesthesia/pathology bills (No Surprises Act covers some but not all elective scenarios).

**The marketing-relevant insight:** for dental, cosmetic, hair, and IVF, US insurance either doesn't cover the procedure or caps out so low that effectively *everyone is cash-pay*. Those categories are the cleanest value proposition. For orthopedic and bariatric, the honest comparison is against US cash rates and high-deductible exposure — not against best-case insured costs.
